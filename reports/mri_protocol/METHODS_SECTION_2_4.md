## 2.4 MRI acquisition protocol

All imaging was performed on a Siemens Prisma 3 T system (syngo MR E11) using a 64-channel head/neck receive coil with participants positioned head-first supine. The session was organized as a single contiguous protocol in which functional, calibration, anatomical and diffusion modules were acquired in a fixed order that is reproducible across the majority of complete visits. Siemens `ProtocolName` labels, DICOM series numbering and BIDS JSON sidecars were used to reconstruct this order; the description below concerns protocol organization rather than numerical pulse-sequence parameters, which are tabulated separately.

The examination opened with a multiplanar gradient-echo localizer used solely for geometric planning. Functional MRI was then collected as one uninterrupted multiband gradient-echo EPI block that shared a common prescription across paradigms. Within that block, four anterior–posterior phase-encoded visually driven task runs were interleaved with a brief posterior–anterior control EPI acquisition, followed by four movie-viewing runs, an opposed-phase-encode spin-echo EPI field-map pair placed between the second and third movie runs, completion of the remaining movie runs, and a final resting-state run acquired with monocular fixation. Matching single-band reference images and physiological log series accompanied the BOLD acquisitions. This arrangement kept stimulus-synchronized and naturalistic visual BOLD measurements contiguous under a stable coil, shim and display configuration, while embedding reverse phase-encode sampling and spin-echo field maps required for susceptibility distortion correction of the functional data.

After the functional block, a second localizer was acquired in most sessions before structural imaging, consistent with re-prescription after prolonged functional scanning. Structural MRI began with a high-resolution T1-weighted MPRAGE that serves as the primary anatomical reference for spatial normalization and morphometric analyses. Short control EPI runs with complementary phase-encode polarities were then collected, followed by a second spin-echo field-map pair. Diffusion imaging followed: a primary multi-shell EPI diffusion series with anterior–posterior phase encoding was paired with a reverse phase-encode b0 acquisition matched in echo time, enabling TOPUP- and eddy-based correction of susceptibility and eddy-current distortions. A turbo-flash B1 map was acquired immediately before a readout-segmented (RESOLVE) diffusion pair in opposed phase-encode directions, providing a lower-distortion diffusion contrast complementary to the multi-shell series. The session concluded with sagittal 3D FLAIR and a white-matter-nulled MPRAGE, supplying CSF-suppressed T2-weighted contrast and enhanced deep-gray T1 contrast, respectively.

Together, the modules are complementary rather than redundant. The shared multiband EPI geometry links task, movie, rest and control BOLD under one distortion model informed by the spin-echo field maps. The reverse phase-encode control and diffusion b0 acquisitions extend that polarity sampling beyond the field maps. Standard T1-weighted MPRAGE anchors anatomical alignment, whereas FLAIR and white-matter-nulled MPRAGE add clinical-research tissue contrasts relevant to white-matter and subcortical evaluation. Multi-shell diffusion supports angularly rich microstructural modelling, while RESOLVE and its reverse phase-encode companion provide a geometrically more faithful diffusion reference. B1 mapping documents transmit-field conditions adjacent to the late diffusion module. Minor visit-level deviations from this canonical order (including occasional swapping of late control phase-encode labels, omission of the second localizer, or early termination before anatomy or diffusion) are recorded in the accompanying session metadata and do not alter the intended modular design of the protocol.
